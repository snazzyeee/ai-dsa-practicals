AI PRACTICAL 9 - AI IMAGE GENERATORS
============================================================
Source: SE_AI_LAB_MANUAL.pdf, assignment 9, PDF pages 55-57.
Revision notes based on the manual; viva questions are study aids.
This is a conceptual study guide, not a record of experiments performed.

AIM
Study text-to-image generation and how prompt design affects visual output.

SAY THIS IN VIVA
An AI image generator creates an image from a description by using
patterns learned during training to produce matching visual content.

CORE THEORY
- Text-to-image: natural-language instructions guide image generation.
- Embedding: a numerical representation of information, such as prompt meaning.
- Latent space: a learned representation where a model can encode information.
- Diffusion: generation by learning to reverse a noise process, often using
  a compressed representation rather than working directly on every pixel.
- GAN: Generative Adversarial Network; a generator and discriminator are
  trained in competition. It is another generative-model approach.
- Style: visual treatment, such as watercolor, cartoon or photorealistic.
- Composition: how subjects and other elements are arranged in the frame.

The manual names DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, Midjourney and Adobe Firefly.
These are examples from the manual, not a current feature or pricing comparison.
Different tools may use different model architectures and interfaces.

WORKING / PRACTICE STEPS
1. Choose the subject and purpose of the image.
2. Describe its setting, style, lighting and composition.
3. Generate the image and inspect whether the details match.
4. Check for artifacts, unwanted content and missing instructions.
5. Refine the prompt and compare results.
6. Save the selected result and explain the changes you requested.

EXAMPLE
Vague: "A cat."
More specific: "A white cat on a wooden windowsill at sunset,
warm lighting, watercolor style, close-up composition."
The second prompt gives more concrete visual direction, though exact
results are not guaranteed. No image-generation experiment is claimed here.

LIKELY VIVA QUESTIONS
Q: Image generation versus image classification?
A: Generation creates an image; classification assigns a label to an image.

Q: Why does changing a prompt change the result?
A: The prompt changes the information conditioning the generation process.

Q: Can the same prompt give different images?
A: Yes. Sampling choices and randomness can produce different results.

Q: Is every image generator a GAN?
A: No. Diffusion and transformer-based approaches are also used.

Q: Common limitations and ethical concerns?
A: Visual artifacts, bias, misleading images, privacy and copyright concerns.

APPLICATIONS AND CONCLUSION
