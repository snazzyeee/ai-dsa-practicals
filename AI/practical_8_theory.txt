Revision notes based on the manual; viva questions are study aids.
This is a conceptual study guide, not a record of experiments performed.

AIM
Study how AI tools turn text prompts into realistic or animated videos.

SAY THIS IN VIVA
AI video generation creates moving visual content from instructions
such as text or images, using learned models of appearance and motion.

CORE THEORY
- Text-to-video: describe a scene and generate a video from it.
- Image-to-video: use an image as a visual starting point for motion.
- Frame: one still image within a video sequence.
- Temporal consistency: maintaining objects, identity and scene details
  coherently across frames as time passes.
- Diffusion models: a model family that can generate content by learning
  to reverse a noise process. Not every video tool uses the same architecture.
- Transformers: architectures used to model relationships in data sequences.

The manual names Runway, Pika, Synthesia, Canva, InVideo and Lumen5.
They illustrate different workflows, including generated scenes, avatars
and assembling videos from assets. They do not all work identically.
Names here are manual examples, not a claim about current plans or features.

WORKING / PRACTICE STEPS
1. Define the subject, action, setting and intended visual style.
2. Add camera motion, lighting and duration where supported.
3. Submit the prompt to the chosen tool and inspect its output.
4. Check motion, scene consistency, visual artifacts and prompt adherence.
5. Refine the prompt and compare results.
6. Save the final result and describe its intended use and limitations.

EXAMPLE PROMPT
"A clay-style bear cooking breakfast in a small kitchen, warm morning
light, fixed camera, gentle movements, five-second scene."
Expected concept: a short animated cooking scene. The actual result
depends on the model and settings; this is not a generated-output report.

LIKELY VIVA QUESTIONS
Q: Why is video generation harder than image generation?
A: It must generate plausible motion and keep multiple frames consistent.

Q: What is a deepfake?
A: Synthetic or altered media that imitates a person's appearance or voice.

Q: What makes a good video prompt?
A: A clear subject, action, setting, style and camera instruction.

Q: Common limitations?
A: Identity drift, unnatural motion, implausible physics and visual artifacts.

Q: What should you evaluate?
A: Prompt match, temporal consistency, motion quality and suitability for use.

APPLICATIONS AND CONCLUSION
Educational visualization, storyboarding, advertising and creative scenes.
Consider consent, bias, misleading content, copyright and disclosure.

REMEMBER: subject + action + setting + camera + consistency.
