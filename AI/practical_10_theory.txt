============================================================
AI PRACTICAL 10 - PROMPT ENGINEERING
============================================================
Source: SE_AI_LAB_MANUAL.pdf, assignment 10, PDF pages 58-60.
Revision notes based on the manual; viva questions are study aids.

AIM
Design and refine prompts to obtain more useful, relevant AI responses.

SAY THIS IN VIVA
Prompt engineering means writing and improving instructions and context
to guide an AI system toward the response required for a task.

CORE THEORY
A useful prompt identifies the task, relevant context, intended audience,
constraints and output format. Examples can demonstrate the desired pattern.
Specific instructions improve clarity but do not guarantee factual accuracy.

TECHNIQUES FROM THE MANUAL
- Zero-shot: request a task without giving examples.
  Example: "Translate 'Good morning' into French."
- Few-shot: give examples of the task before asking for another answer.
  Example: "happy -> positive; terrible -> negative; excellent -> ?"
- Role prompting: specify a useful viewpoint or teaching role.
  Example: "Act as a beginner-friendly tutor and explain queues."
- Constraint-based: specify limits such as length, tone or format.
  Example: "Explain BFS in three points, using at most 80 words."
- Step-by-step explanation: ask for an understandable solution outline.
  The manual calls this chain-of-thought prompting. For revision, request
  key steps and a checked answer; a generated explanation is not proof
  of correctness or a direct view of a model's internal reasoning.

WORKING / PRACTICE STEPS
1. Define the result you need and the audience receiving it.
2. Write the task and supply relevant source material or context.
3. Add format, length and other constraints.
4. Add examples if the desired pattern is otherwise unclear.
5. Inspect the response for relevance, correctness and instruction following.
6. Revise the unclear part of the prompt and compare responses.

EXAMPLE
Vague: "Explain AI."
Improved: "Explain artificial intelligence to a first-year student in
100 words. Give two everyday examples and avoid technical jargon."

LIKELY VIVA QUESTIONS
Q: Zero-shot versus few-shot?
A: Zero-shot provides no examples; few-shot supplies example inputs and outputs.

Q: Prompting versus fine-tuning?
A: Prompting changes the input context; fine-tuning updates model weights
   through additional training.

Q: Why use role prompting?
A: It helps specify the viewpoint, language and depth of the response.

Q: Can a good prompt eliminate hallucinations?
A: No. Verify important claims and provide reliable source material where useful.

Q: How do you judge whether a prompt improved?
A: Compare correctness, relevance, clarity and adherence to the requested format.

APPLICATIONS AND CONCLUSION
Tutoring, summarization, drafting, coding and image/video instructions.
Prompt engineering is iterative: write, inspect, revise and verify.

REMEMBER: task + context + audience + constraints + format + review.
