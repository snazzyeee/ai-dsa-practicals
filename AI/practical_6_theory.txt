============================================================
AI PRACTICAL 6 - MINIMAX WITH ALPHA-BETA PRUNING
============================================================
Source: SE_AI_LAB_MANUAL.pdf, assignment 6, PDF pages 41-48.
Revision notes based on the manual; viva questions are study aids.

AIM
Reduce minimax evaluations by pruning branches that cannot affect the decision.

SAY THIS IN VIVA
Alpha-beta pruning improves minimax by skipping branches that cannot
change the final minimax value. It keeps the same optimal result.

CORE THEORY
- Alpha: the best score MAX can already guarantee; a lower bound.
- Beta: the best score MIN can already guarantee; an upper bound.
- Initialize alpha to negative infinity and beta to positive infinity.
- MAX updates alpha using the maximum score seen so far.
- MIN updates beta using the minimum score seen so far.
- Prune when alpha >= beta, equivalently beta <= alpha.

Pruning saves work because a player already has an alternative that is
at least as good. It does not mean the skipped branch is invalid.
Different move orders change how much can be pruned, not the minimax value.
Equally good moves may be selected differently when move order changes.

WORKING / ALGORITHM
1. Check for a terminal state and return its utility.
2. At a MAX node, evaluate children and update best and alpha.
3. At a MIN node, evaluate children and update best and beta.
4. Pass the current bounds down the recursive search.
5. Stop checking remaining children when alpha >= beta.
6. Undo trial moves and return the best value found at this node.

EXAMPLE
MAX already has a move worth 3, so alpha is 3.
In another branch, MIN finds a child worth 2, so beta becomes 2.
MIN's result there can only stay at 2 or decrease as more children are checked.
Since beta <= alpha, MAX will not prefer that branch; skip its remaining children.

LIKELY VIVA QUESTIONS
Q: Main difference from ordinary minimax?
A: The evaluation logic is the same, but irrelevant branches are skipped.

Q: What is the pruning condition?
A: alpha >= beta.

Q: Does pruning change the optimal minimax score?
A: No, when applied correctly to the same search tree and evaluation.

Q: Best-case and worst-case time?
A: With excellent move ordering, O(b^(d/2)); worst case remains O(b^d).

Q: Why does move ordering matter?
A: Finding strong moves early creates tighter bounds and more cutoffs.

APPLICATIONS AND CONCLUSION
Game-tree search, including the manual's Tic-Tac-Toe example.
Pruning makes deeper search practical without changing the decision rule.

REMEMBER: MAX raises alpha; MIN lowers beta; prune when they meet or cross.
