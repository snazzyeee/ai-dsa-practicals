============================================================
AI PRACTICAL 4 - CONSTRAINT SATISFACTION PROBLEM (CSP)
============================================================
Source: SE_AI_LAB_MANUAL.pdf, assignment 4, PDF pages 27-32.
Revision notes based on the manual; viva questions are study aids.

AIM
Solve map coloring using backtracking with constraint propagation.
The manual uses Australian regions, MRV and the AC-3 algorithm.

SAY THIS IN VIVA
A CSP assigns values to variables from their domains so that all
constraints are satisfied.

CORE THEORY
- Variables: things to assign, such as map regions WA, NT and SA.
- Domains: possible values, such as red, green and blue.
- Constraints: restrictions, such as adjacent regions having different colors.
- Assignment: a chosen value for a variable.
- Backtracking: undo a failed choice and try another value.
- MRV: Minimum Remaining Values; choose the variable with the fewest
  currently legal values, exposing difficult choices early.
- Constraint propagation: remove domain values ruled out by constraints.

AC-3 / ARC CONSISTENCY
For a directed arc X -> Y, every remaining value of X must have at least
one supporting value of Y that satisfies the constraint between them.
AC-3 repeatedly removes unsupported values and revisits affected arcs.
If a domain becomes empty, that branch cannot produce a solution.
Arc consistency alone does not guarantee that the whole CSP is solvable.

WORKING / ALGORITHM
1. Define the regions, their colors and neighboring regions.
2. If all regions are assigned, return the solution.
3. Choose an unassigned region using MRV.
4. Try a color consistent with already assigned neighbors.
5. Restrict its domain to that color and run AC-3 on copied domains.
6. Recurse if propagation succeeds; otherwise undo the choice.
7. Try another color, or backtrack if none works.

EXAMPLE
If WA is red and WA borders NT, remove red from NT's possible colors.
NT can still be green or blue. If no color remains, undo an earlier choice.

LIKELY VIVA QUESTIONS
Q: What are the three parts of a CSP?
A: Variables, domains and constraints.

Q: Why use propagation with backtracking?
A: It detects impossible choices earlier and reduces unnecessary search.

Q: What does AC-3 stand for?
A: Arc Consistency Algorithm 3.

Q: Why copy domains before trying a color?
A: So failed branches do not permanently remove values needed by other branches.

Q: Other CSP examples?
A: Sudoku, timetable scheduling and the N-Queens problem.

APPLICATIONS AND CONCLUSION
CSPs model problems through restrictions rather than a fixed sequence of moves.
Backtracking searches; MRV and AC-3 reduce the search needed.

REMEMBER: variables + domains + constraints; assign, propagate, backtrack.
